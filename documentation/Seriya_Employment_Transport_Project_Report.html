Executive Summary

Seriya is a proposed employment transport management system designed to improve the reliability, visibility and coordination of daily employee transport. Many employment transport services depend on telephone calls, messaging groups and manual attendance lists. These methods make it difficult for passengers to know where their vehicle is, for drivers to know who is travelling and where to stop, and for administrators to monitor vehicles across multiple routes.

The proposed solution consists of a Flutter mobile application for employee passengers and drivers, supported by a separate web administration dashboard. Passengers will declare whether they are travelling, provide or confirm the relevant pickup or drop-off point, and view the latest available location of their assigned vehicle. Drivers will see the passengers expected for the current shift, their stop locations and their attendance status. Administrators will create routes, manage users and vehicles, control assignments, and track active vehicles from a central dashboard.

Every operating day contains two independently managed transport shifts:

  • Morning inbound trip: the vehicle collects passengers from their approved pickup points and transports them to the office.
  • Evening outbound trip: the vehicle collects passengers from the office and drops them at their approved destinations.

Attendance and trip progress must be stored separately for the morning and evening shifts because a passenger may travel in only one direction on a given day. Vehicle location sharing is active only while a driver has started a trip, helping protect privacy and battery life. The first release will focus on core coordination and tracking, while route optimization, automated ETA calculation, payments, advanced reporting and other enhancements can be added later.

Expected outcome: Passengers receive better visibility and arrive on time, drivers receive a reliable shift-specific stop list, and administrators gain a real-time operational view of the transport fleet.

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

Employee transport is a recurring operational service where a vehicle follows an assigned route to carry employees between approved locations and the workplace. The service depends on timely information: passengers need to know when the vehicle is approaching, drivers need an accurate list of expected passengers, and administrators need to know whether assigned vehicles are operating normally.

1.2 Problem Statement

Without a shared digital system, daily transport coordination can produce uncertainty, delays and unnecessary communication. A driver may stop for a passenger who is absent, a passenger may miss a vehicle because its progress is unknown, and an administrator may only learn about a delay after receiving complaints. Pickup details may also be distributed across messages rather than maintained as controlled assignment data.

1.3 Project Aim

To design and implement a secure cross-platform system that coordinates employee attendance, passenger stops, driver trips, vehicle assignments and live vehicle visibility for morning and evening employment transport services.

1.4 Objectives

  • Provide passengers with the latest available location of their assigned vehicle during active trips.
  • Allow passengers to submit separate morning and evening attendance.
  • Allow passengers to provide or confirm approved pickup and drop-off locations.
  • Provide drivers with a shift-specific passenger and stop list.
  • Allow drivers to start and end trips and report pickup outcomes.
  • Allow administrators to manage users, vehicles, routes and assignments.
  • Allow administrators to monitor all active vehicles from a web dashboard.
  • Create a modular foundation for later features without redesigning the entire application.

  • Automated route optimization and dynamic re-routing.
  • Transport fee collection, payroll deductions and payment processing.
  • Public transport or third-party fleet marketplace integration.
  • Vehicle maintenance scheduling and fuel management.
  • Advanced predictive ETA, traffic analytics and geofenced automation.
  • In-app chat or voice communication.
  • Biometric attendance verification.
  • Multi-company tenancy unless specifically approved as an initial requirement.

2.3 Assumptions

  • Each operating day normally has one morning and one evening shift.
  • A passenger may attend both shifts, only one shift, or neither shift.
  • Passengers normally use an assigned route and vehicle, but administrators can change assignments.
  • The office is the common morning destination and evening origin for the initial deployment.
  • The driver uses a GPS-capable Android or iOS phone with mobile data.
  • Maps and cloud services require internet connectivity, although limited cached data can support temporary disruptions.
  • Business-specific cutoff times, retention periods and escalation rules will be confirmed before production deployment.

4. Daily Transport Operating Model

4.1 Trip Types

TripOrigin and destinationPassenger location usedAttendance record
Morning inboundApproved passenger pickup points to the office.Morning pickup point.Morning attendance for that operating date.
Evening outboundThe office to approved passenger drop-off points.Evening drop-off point, which may match the morning point.Evening attendance for that operating date.
Important: Morning and evening attendance must never be treated as one combined daily value. An employee may arrive independently but use company transport in the evening, or use morning transport and leave by another method.

4.2 Standard Daily Lifecycle

Trips scheduled→Passengers submit attendance→Driver reviews stops
Driver starts trip→Live location published→Pickups/drop-offs recorded
Driver ends trip→Tracking stops→Trip retained in history

4.3 Morning Workflow

  1. The system creates or exposes the scheduled morning trip.
  2. Each passenger marks Travelling or Not travelling before the configured morning cutoff.
  3. The driver reviews only passengers expected for the morning trip and their pickup points.
  4. The driver starts the morning trip; live location sharing begins.
  5. Assigned passengers can view the latest vehicle position and freshness timestamp.
  6. The driver records each passenger as picked up, absent/no-show or skipped with a reason.
  7. After arriving at the office, the driver ends the trip and location publishing stops.

4.4 Evening Workflow

  1. The evening trip remains separate from the completed morning trip.
  2. Passengers submit evening attendance before the evening cutoff.
  3. The driver sees employees travelling from the office and their approved drop-off points.
  4. The driver starts the evening trip at the office; live tracking begins.
  5. The driver records drop-off progress or relevant exceptions.
  6. After completing the route, the driver ends the evening trip and tracking stops.

4.5 Trip States

scheduled → ready → active → completed

Exception states may include cancelled and interrupted. Every state change should store the actor and timestamp for audit purposes.

8.2 Key Relationships

  • A route contains many ordered stops.
  • A vehicle may be assigned to a route for an effective period.
  • A driver may be assigned to a vehicle for an effective period.
  • A passenger is assigned to a route/vehicle and approved morning/evening stops.
  • Each service date produces a morning trip and an evening trip where scheduled.
  • Each trip has many trip-passenger records, even when a passenger declares absence.
  • Each active trip has one latest-location record and may optionally have a limited history subject to policy.

11. Location Tracking Design

11.1 Tracking Lifecycle

  1. The driver signs in and opens an assigned scheduled trip.
  2. The app checks location permission, GPS availability, device connectivity and trip assignment.
  3. The driver explicitly starts the trip.
  4. The device sends bounded location updates associated with the active trip and vehicle.
  5. Authorized clients listen only to the required vehicle location path.
  6. The interface displays the recorded time, accuracy where useful and a stale indicator.
  7. When the trip ends, continuous publishing stops and the last record is marked inactive.

11.2 Accuracy and Freshness

Mobile GPS cannot guarantee an exact point. Buildings, weather, device settings, tunnels, power-saving modes and network conditions can affect accuracy and delivery time. Therefore, the system should display the latest available position with a timestamp and consider the marker stale after a configurable threshold, initially proposed as 30–60 seconds.

11.3 Battery and Data Use

Location frequency should adapt to the operational need. Updates can be more frequent while the vehicle is moving and less frequent when stationary. The driver should always see whether tracking is active. The MVP should store only the latest position unless route history is explicitly approved; if history is required, sampling and retention must be bounded.

11.4 Safety

The driver interface must minimize interaction while driving. Route preparation and passenger review should occur before movement. Navigation should be delegated to an appropriate navigation experience where adopted, and the app should not encourage manual map interaction while the vehicle is moving.

13. Business Rules and Validation

  1. Only an authorized administrator may approve roles and transport assignments.
  2. A passenger's morning and evening attendance are separate records.
  3. A passenger can submit attendance only for permitted dates and before the applicable cutoff, unless an audited override is used.
  4. A passenger must not see vehicles or passenger information outside the assigned service.
  5. A driver can start only a trip assigned to that driver and vehicle.
  6. A vehicle and driver should not have conflicting active trips.
  7. The trip type determines whether the passenger's morning pickup or evening drop-off point is used.
  8. Location publishing requires an active trip and stops when the trip ends.
  9. Stale location data must be labelled and never presented as current.
  10. Vehicle capacity conflicts should be shown to administrators during assignment.
  11. Disabled users cannot access protected operational data.
  12. Privileged overrides must store the administrator, reason and timestamp.

14.2 Critical Scenarios

  • A passenger attends morning only and is excluded correctly from the evening driver list.
  • A passenger attends evening only and appears with the correct drop-off location.
  • A pending or disabled account cannot access trip data.
  • A driver cannot start another driver's trip.
  • A passenger cannot query another route's live location or stops.
  • Location becomes stale when the driver loses connectivity and is clearly labelled.
  • Ending a trip stops publishing and removes active visibility as required.
  • Offline passenger/driver changes synchronize without duplicating or corrupting attendance.
  • An administrator assignment conflict is rejected with a clear explanation.

15. Deployment and Operations

15.1 Environments

  • Development: developer testing and local emulators.
  • Staging: realistic test users, vehicles and routes without production personal data.
  • Production: approved users and live operations with restricted administrative access.

15.2 Release Approach

  1. Run code analysis, automated tests and security-rule tests.
  2. Build signed Android and iOS release candidates.
  3. Conduct a controlled pilot with one route and a small user group.
  4. Review GPS reliability, attendance compliance, battery impact and support issues.
  5. Correct pilot findings before wider rollout.
  6. Publish through the approved mobile distribution channels and deploy the admin web dashboard to a secured domain.

15.3 Operational Monitoring

Monitor authentication failures, backend errors, crash-free sessions, notification delivery, stale active vehicles and unusual administrative activity. Alerts should avoid including sensitive personal or precise location data unless necessary and access-controlled.

  2. What are the attendance submission cutoffs for each shift?
  3. Can passengers change attendance after the cutoff, and who approves the change?
  4. Does each passenger have the same morning pickup and evening drop-off point?
  5. Can a passenger request a temporary stop for a single day?

20. MVP Acceptance Criteria

The MVP can be considered functionally complete when all of the following are demonstrated in staging and accepted by stakeholders:

  • A user can register, but cannot access operational data until approved.
  • An administrator can approve users and assign passengers, drivers, vehicles and routes.
  • The system creates or manages separate morning and evening trips for the same date.
  • A passenger can independently declare morning and evening attendance.
  • The driver sees only the correct passengers and stops for the selected shift.
  • The driver can start a valid trip and the assigned passenger can see the latest vehicle location.
  • An administrator can see all authorized active vehicles and their freshness state.
  • The driver can record passenger outcomes and end the trip.
  • Location publishing stops after trip completion.
  • Unauthorized cross-route and privileged access attempts are rejected by backend rules.
  • Core workflows pass on supported Android and iOS devices and the admin dashboard works in approved browsers.
  • Privacy, retention, account approval and operational policies have been approved.

Conclusion

Seriya addresses a clear daily coordination problem by giving each participant the information appropriate to their role. Separating the day into a morning inbound trip and an evening outbound trip is central to the design: each shift has its own attendance, stop use, driver workflow, trip status and tracking lifecycle. A Flutter mobile application, a separate administration dashboard and a secured real-time backend provide a practical MVP architecture. The design remains intentionally modular so later capabilities such as ETA, optimization, notifications, reporting and HR integration can be introduced without changing the core role and trip model.
